💡 Conclusions & Recommendations — How to Reduce Deleted Posts

▶

High overall deletion rate (24.2%) — More than 1 in 5 posts is removed. This suggests structural issues with content quality or moderation thresholds.

▶

Slow deletion pipeline (avg 219 days) — Posts linger before removal. Real-time auto-flagging could accelerate the pipeline.

▶

Closed → Deleted pipeline takes avg 95 days — Consider an automated rule to delete posts that remain closed without improvement after a defined SLA window.

▶

New/low-reputation users drive the majority of deleted posts — Implementing a stricter first-post review queue and reputation-gated posting privileges would directly reduce deletion volume.

▶

Country-level patterns exist — Certain regions show consistently higher deletion rates. Localized guidance, translated FAQs, and region-specific moderation bots are high-ROI interventions.

▶

Tag-level risk signals — Some tags are disproportionately associated with deleted posts. Tag-specific posting templates and automated warnings at submission time could reduce off-topic or low-quality content.

▶

Temporal clustering — Deletion spikes on specific days/hours suggest moderation team activity patterns or bot-driven spam windows. Scheduling automated scans during off-peak hours would complement human review capacity.

▶

Review pipeline effectiveness — Cross-reference review rejection reasons with deletion reasons to identify which review types most reliably predict deletion, and prioritize those queues.

📌 Suggested Next Steps:
1. Implement a quality gate at submission for users with reputation < 10.
2. Build an automated close → delete SLA rule (e.g. 7 days with no edit).
3. Deploy tag-specific post templates for the top 5 high-risk tags.
4. Create a localized moderation playbook for the top 3 high-deletion countries.
5. Schedule bot-assisted spam scans during the peak deletion hours identified.
